Electric Car Charging Cost in Pakistan – Home vs Petrol

Updated October 2026 • EV Charging Cost Pakistan | Tesla Charging Cost

With rising petrol prices across Pakistan, more people are looking towards electric vehicles as a long term solution. One of the most common questions asked by new EV buyers is simple: what is the actual EV charging cost Pakistan drivers can expect when charging at home? The answer is surprisingly affordable when compared to filling up a petrol car for the same distance.

For most households that install a home charger or simply use a dedicated wall socket, charging remains by far the cheapest way to run an electric car. In this guide, we take a realistic look at the Tesla charging cost at home, compare it with petrol and show you practical ways to bring your monthly running cost down even further.

Home Charging Cost Calculation in Pakistan

To keep things practical, we will use the Tesla Model Y as a real world example. This model is becoming increasingly common for Pakistan imports, so the numbers are relevant for many buyers considering it.

On average, households in Pakistan pay around PKR 50 per unit (kWh) for electricity at home. A Tesla Model Y with a typical charge from low state of charge to full usually requires around 75 units. Based on this, the full charge cost comes out to 75 x 50 = PKR 3,750. With this full charge, most owners can easily cover around 400 km of mixed driving depending on weather, speed and load.

This means the effective cost for 400 km comes down to roughly PKR 9.38 per km when charging at home, which is one of the lowest running costs you can get for a family SUV.

It is worth noting that public DC fast chargers are convenient on road trips, but they almost always cost more per unit. For daily use, home charging will almost always be the winner for keeping your EV charging cost Pakistan low.

How to Reduce Charging Cost

Even though home charging is already cheap, there are a few smart ways to bring your Tesla charging cost down even further.

  1. Charge during off-peak hours: If your DISCO offers time of use or lower night rates, schedule charging late at night to save more per unit.
  2. Avoid unnecessary top ups: You do not need to charge to 100% every day. Keeping between 20% – 80% is efficient for most daily commutes and is great for long term battery health.
  3. Use solar if possible: If you have net metering or a solar setup, charging directly from your own generation can drastically cut your effective EV charging cost Pakistan down to next to nothing.

Other Important Considerations

Rates can shift over time depending on fuel adjustments and electricity tariffs. Some housing societies may have different rules for EV chargers. Also, if you rely purely on public charging for most of your top ups, your per km cost will be higher than the home based numbers shown above. For the true minimum running cost, a dedicated home connection is the way to go.

Overall, for anyone looking at long term savings, switching to an EV makes a lot of sense in Pakistan right now, especially with petrol prices staying high.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q1. How much does it cost to charge a Tesla at home in Pakistan?

A: For a Tesla Model Y, around 75 units are needed for a full charge. At roughly PKR 50 per unit, that comes to about PKR 3,750 for ~400 km.

Q2. Is EV charging cheaper than petrol in Pakistan?

A: Yes. Home EV charging is usually 60–70% cheaper than running a comparable petrol SUV for the same distance.

Q3. Is public charging more expensive than home charging?

A: Yes. Public DC fast chargers often charge higher per unit compared to home electricity rates, so home charging remains the most affordable option for daily use.
