Tesla FSD Subscription Cost in 2026: Is $99 Per Month Worth It?

A practical look at the monthly price, what the software can do, what it cannot do, and how to decide whether a subscription fits your driving.

Tesla FSD subscription cost in 2026 is listed at $99 per month in the United States for Full Self-Driving (Supervised). That headline price can make the feature sound like an easy add-on, but whether it is worth paying for depends on your vehicle's eligibility, how often you drive, the roads you use and how much value you place on its driver-assistance features.

There is one point to understand before comparing prices: Tesla calls the system “Full Self-Driving (Supervised),” and it is not an autonomous-driving service. Drivers must remain attentive and responsible for the vehicle at all times. This guide explains the subscription math, core features, limitations, eligibility checks and a practical way to decide whether to keep paying.

Quick answer: how much is Tesla FSD in 2026?

Tesla's US FSD page and support information list the monthly subscription at $99 USD per month. Tesla notes that prices and feature availability can change, and the features available to a particular owner depend on the vehicle and market. Always confirm the price shown in your Tesla app or vehicle before subscribing.

For a driver who wants to try FSD on a road trip or during a busy month, a monthly subscription may be easier to evaluate than treating it as a permanent ownership cost. Someone who rarely uses the features may decide that the recurring fee is not worthwhile.

What does FSD (Supervised) include?

Tesla describes FSD (Supervised) as a collection of advanced driver-assistance features. Depending on the vehicle, software version, location and regulatory approvals, supported functions can include:

  • Route-based driving assistance: the system can follow navigation routes and perform supported driving maneuvers while the driver supervises.
  • Lane changes: it can move into neighboring lanes when the feature is available and conditions permit.
  • Turns and intersections: it can handle supported left and right turns and route choices under supervision.
  • Autopark: the vehicle can assist with parallel or perpendicular parking when the feature is supported.
  • Summon-related features: availability varies by vehicle, software and market; check the feature list for your specific car.

Do not assume every item is available on every Tesla. Tesla's support pages explain that feature access varies by configuration and location, and some capabilities depend on regulatory approval. The in-car software and Tesla app are the best places to verify what your vehicle can currently use.

What FSD does not do: important safety limits

The word “Self-Driving” can create the wrong expectation. Tesla explicitly says currently enabled FSD (Supervised) features do not make the vehicle autonomous. A fully attentive driver must keep watching the road, remain ready to take over immediately and follow traffic laws.

  • Keep your eyes on the road and your attention on the driving task.
  • Be ready to steer, brake or accelerate at any moment.
  • Never use a phone or treat the system as a substitute for an attentive driver.
  • Do not assume the vehicle will correctly handle every road layout, construction zone, emergency vehicle, weather condition or unusual obstacle.
  • Use the feature only where it is available and lawful, and follow all instructions and warnings from the vehicle.

Driver-assistance systems can make mistakes. Road markings may be unclear, visibility can be poor, and unusual traffic situations can require human judgment. If the system behaves unexpectedly, take control safely. The driver remains legally and practically responsible for the vehicle.

Is $99 per month worth it? A practical way to decide

There is no universal yes-or-no answer. Instead of focusing only on the monthly price, think about the number of days you drive, the types of roads you use and whether the supported features meaningfully reduce workload without making you less attentive.

A simple test is to subscribe for one month only if your vehicle is eligible, use the feature in appropriate conditions, and then review how often you actually used it. Ask yourself whether it was useful, whether you stayed comfortable supervising it and whether you would willingly pay another $99. Do not keep a subscription just because you feel you should use every feature included with your car.

How to subscribe to Tesla FSD

Tesla's support instructions say eligible owners can subscribe through the Tesla app or the vehicle touchscreen. In the app, the general path is:

  1. Open the Tesla app associated with the account that owns the vehicle.
  2. Tap Upgrades, then Software Upgrades.
  3. Look for Full Self-Driving (Supervised).
  4. Review the displayed price, terms and any eligibility or hardware requirements.
  5. If you choose to proceed, follow the app's subscription confirmation steps.

The exact labels can change with app updates. You can also check the vehicle touchscreen under Controls and Upgrades if the subscription option is offered for your car. Tesla says a software update may be needed before the features become available.

Which Tesla vehicles are eligible?

Do not rely on a generic model-year list when checking compatibility. Tesla says eligibility depends on vehicle configuration and hardware; some cars may need a prerequisite hardware upgrade at an additional cost. Feature availability can also vary by region and regulatory approval.

Check the Upgrades section in the Tesla app while signed in to the account that owns the vehicle. If the FSD option appears, read any compatibility notes and additional upgrade costs before subscribing. If it does not appear, contact Tesla support rather than assuming the vehicle can be enabled by paying the monthly fee alone.

Can you cancel Tesla FSD at any time?

Tesla's subscription support page says owners can cancel through the Tesla app. The general path is Upgrades → Manage → Full Self-Driving (Supervised) → Cancel Subscription. Tesla states that access continues for the remainder of the current billing period after cancellation is processed.

One billing detail matters: Tesla says subscription payments are not prorated. If you cancel partway through a billing period, do not expect a partial-month refund. Check the renewal date and the terms displayed in your account before confirming a subscription or cancellation.
