Tesla Model Y Depreciation: 2026 Resale Value

A data-led guide to used Model Y prices, depreciation estimates and the factors that can move your resale value up or down.

Tesla Model Y depreciation in 2026 is an important part of the ownership equation. The purchase price is only the beginning: if you sell or trade in the vehicle later, the difference between what you paid and what a buyer will offer can be one of your largest ownership costs. The challenge is that there is no single resale figure that applies to every Model Y. Trim, mileage, condition, new-car pricing, incentives and used-EV demand all matter.

For a useful benchmark, iSeeCars' resale-value analysis estimates that a Tesla Model Y loses about 44.1% of its value after three years and 55.4% after five years. Those are model-level estimates from its dataset, not a promise about what your specific car will sell for. Another estimator, CarEdge, reports a different five-year estimate, which is a good reminder that methods and assumptions produce different forecasts.

Quick answer
Benchmark estimate: about 44% depreciation after 3 years and 55% after 5 years in the iSeeCars analysis. Your result may be substantially different. Use a current local-market quote before making a buying or selling decision.

What do those percentages mean in dollars?

Depreciation is the difference between a vehicle's starting value and its later resale value. For example, if a Model Y originally cost $40,000 before taxes and fees, a 44.1% decline would imply a remaining value of roughly $22,360. A 55.4% decline would imply roughly $17,840. These are arithmetic examples using a hypothetical $40,000 starting value; they are not live quotes and do not include taxes, financing, delivery fees or optional equipment.

Why does the Tesla Model Y lose value?

1. New Tesla prices and incentives

When the price of a new Model Y falls, used examples often need to become more affordable to compete. Manufacturer discounts, financing offers, inventory incentives and trim changes can all affect used-car pricing. A buyer comparing a nearly new used Model Y with a new one will look at the full out-the-door cost, not just the used listing price.

2. Model refreshes and feature changes

Design updates, range improvements, interior revisions, hardware changes and new software capabilities can make newer model years more attractive. A refresh does not automatically make an older Model Y a poor purchase, but it can change what buyers are willing to pay for earlier versions.

3. Mileage and vehicle condition

Annual mileage, tire wear, paint and interior condition, windshield damage, accident history and repair quality influence buyer confidence. A well-kept vehicle with clear records is easier to evaluate than one with incomplete history or visible damage. Mileage is only one part of the picture, but it is an important comparison point.

4. Battery health and warranty coverage

Battery condition matters to used-EV shoppers, but a battery-health percentage should not be guessed from the odometer. Provide genuine vehicle information when available, keep warranty and repair documents, and let a qualified inspection or a credible battery report support any claims. Remaining battery and drive-unit warranty can also influence buyer confidence; verify coverage for the exact vehicle and original in-service date.

5. Used-EV supply and buyer demand

Used values respond to local inventory, interest rates, fuel prices, electricity costs, tax rules and consumer confidence. A Model Y may command a different price in one metro area than another. National averages are useful for context, but local comparable listings and firm purchase offers are more relevant when you are ready to sell.

How to estimate your Model Y's resale value accurately

  1. Identify the exact configuration. Record model year, trim, drivetrain, wheels, options and any hardware differences.
  2. Use the current odometer reading. Compare vehicles with similar mileage rather than relying only on year.
  3. Check condition and history. Note accident records, repairs, tire condition, cosmetic wear and any warning messages.
  4. Compare several local sources. Review comparable dealer listings and request actual purchase or trade-in offers from more than one buyer.
  5. Separate asking prices from sold prices. A listing is what a seller hopes to receive, not proof of the final transaction amount.
  6. Check the loan payoff. If you financed the vehicle, compare the offer with your remaining loan balance to understand whether you have positive or negative equity.

For a useful estimate, look at the same trim and model year within a reasonable mileage range. Avoid comparing a low-mileage Long Range vehicle with a high-mileage base trim and treating the price gap as depreciation alone.

How to slow depreciation and protect resale value

  • Keep the vehicle clean and repair small cosmetic problems before they become larger issues.
  • Maintain tire pressure and alignment, and keep receipts for tire replacement and other work.
  • Save purchase documents, repair invoices, recall-completion records and warranty information.
  • Avoid unnecessary modifications that narrow the pool of potential buyers.
  • Keep charging habits and battery-related claims factual; never advertise an unverified battery-health figure.
  • Before selling, compare private-sale, dealer trade-in and online instant-offer prices.
  • Choose sale timing based on your needs and real market offers rather than assuming a specific month guarantees a higher price.

Should you buy a new or used Model Y in 2026?

A new Model Y gives you the latest configuration, a new-vehicle warranty timeline and the ability to choose the trim and options you want. A used Model Y may offer a lower entry price because an earlier owner has already absorbed part of the initial depreciation. However, the used vehicle's remaining warranty, mileage, condition, accident history, tire wear and exact hardware should be checked carefully.

Compare the total cost, not only the sticker price. Include insurance, electricity, registration, financing and expected maintenance. Frequently asked questions

How much does a Tesla Model Y depreciate after five years?

iSeeCars estimates about 55.4% depreciation after five years in its published Model Y resale-value analysis. This is a statistical estimate, not a guaranteed value for a specific vehicle.

What is the Model Y's three-year depreciation?

iSeeCars reports about 44.1% depreciation after three years. Different valuation services may produce different results because they use different data and assumptions.

Can a Tesla Model Y lose value quickly after a price cut?

It can. A lower new-car price or stronger incentive can put pressure on comparable used-car prices, although the effect depends on the model, trim, timing and local market.

Does battery health determine resale value?

Battery condition can influence buyer confidence, but it is not the only factor. Mileage, warranty, accident history, trim, tires, condition and local supply also matter. Use verified information rather than estimates.

Is it better to sell a Model Y after three or five years?

There is no universal best point. Compare current offers, loan payoff, expected future costs and your replacement-car plans. A model-level depreciation curve cannot tell you the best sale date for your own situation.
