Tesla Software Update 2026: FSD 14.2, 14.3.13 & Emergency Drive Away

Tesla’s November software releases bring a mix of supervised-driving improvements, safety-related behavior and everyday convenience features. Here is how to understand FSD 14.2, the newer 14.3.13 rollout and Emergency Drive Away—without confusing a software update with a self-driving car.

Feature image: Tesla software update guide for US owners.

Tesla software updates can change how a vehicle navigates, parks, charges and presents information on its center display. In early November 2026, two release-note versions are worth separating: software 2026.39.6, which lists FSD 14.2.2.6, and an early rollout of 2026.38.5.12, which lists FSD 14.3.13. They are separate software builds, not one combined update. Which one appears on a particular car depends on rollout timing, model, hardware and region.

This guide focuses on the features US owners are likely to search for, including Emergency Drive Away, supervised Full Self-Driving, collision-evasion behavior and practical ways to check update availability. Always use the release notes shown for your own vehicle as the final reference.

FSD 14.2 and 14.3: understand the different releases

The FSD number is only one part of a Tesla software version. A release can be identified by its vehicle software build (for example, 2026.39.6) and the FSD package version shown in its notes. A car may receive a feature update on a different schedule from another car, even if both are the same broad model.

If your Software screen shows a different version, that does not automatically mean something is wrong.

Emergency Drive Away: a feature for exceptional situations

One of the more unusual additions is Emergency Drive Away. The release notes describe it as allowing the driver to shift into Drive while a Supercharger cable is still connected. It is designed for an emergency situation—not as a faster way to leave a charging stall.

Important safety warning: Tesla’s release notes warn that driving away with the charging cable attached can damage both the vehicle and the Supercharger, and the driver may be responsible for repair costs. Use the function only when an actual emergency makes it necessary.

The feature also has a privacy implication: the release notes say short recordings from the vehicle’s cameras are shared with Tesla when the function is activated. Owners should read the on-screen explanation and applicable privacy information before using it.

Availability is limited by model and software build. The November 5 release notes for 2026.38.5.12 specifically list Emergency Drive Away as available only in the United States. The October 29 2026.39.6 notes list it across supported newer Model S, Model 3, Model X, Model Y and Cybertruck configurations. Confirm eligibility in the release notes for your own vehicle.

FSD (Supervised): what it can and cannot do

Tesla describes Full Self-Driving (Supervised) as a driver-assistance feature that can handle tasks such as following a navigation route, making lane changes and turns, navigating around other road users and stopping at a destination. Depending on the supported vehicle, it can also assist with parking-related maneuvers.

The word Supervised matters. Tesla explicitly says the system does not make the vehicle autonomous. The driver must remain attentive, monitor the road and be ready to take over. It is not permission to use a phone, sleep, leave the driver’s seat or treat the car as an independent robotaxi.

In the 2026.39.6 notes, Tesla instructs eligible drivers to watch a tutorial and take a quiz in the Self-Driving app or under Controls > Self-Driving before starting the feature. Controls can vary by model and software version. Follow the instructions on your own screen and read Tesla’s FSD (Supervised) support page.

Other notable features in the November software notes

Automatic Collision Evasion

The 2026.38.5.12 release notes describe Automatic Collision Evasion as a system that can activate FSD (Supervised) in an attempt to help keep the vehicle safe and continue driving if it detects a situation in which the driver may have become insufficiently attentive and FSD may have been unintentionally disengaged. The notes list supported AI4 vehicles. This is not a guarantee that a collision will be prevented.

Set Arrival Energy

The 2026.39.6 notes describe setting a desired arrival battery level from the Tesla mobile app. An owner can enter a destination in the location tab and choose Set Arrival Energy. The notes state that mobile app version 4.58.0 or later is required. Actual energy use still varies with speed, weather, elevation and climate control.

Dynamic Headlight Leveling

On supported vehicles, low-beam headlights can automatically adjust their aim based on driving conditions and nearby traffic. The stated goal is to improve visibility while reducing glare for other road users. The release notes list selected Model 3 and Cybertruck configurations, so do not assume every Tesla receives it.

Supercharger site maps

The 2026.39.6 notes describe detailed maps for select Supercharger locations. Where supported, drivers can tap View Site Map to see the site layout and live stall status such as available, occupied or down. The feature is not necessarily present at every charging site.

Apple Music and visual changes

The early 2026.38.5.12 rollout also lists an Apple Music library navigation update for supported vehicles. The 2026.39.6 notes include visual changes such as higher-quality vehicle images and an enhanced parking scene on certain configurations, plus Halloween Mode. These additions can vary by display computer and vehicle generation.

Which Tesla models get the features?

There is no single compatibility list that applies to every feature. Release notes identify eligible models and sometimes narrow support further by hardware generation, trim or region. A feature can be listed for Model 3 and Model Y but still be limited to AI4 hardware, or appear on a newer Model S refresh but not an older generation.

  • Check the exact software build: compare the number on your car with the release-note version.
  • Check the hardware line: notes may specify AI3, AI4, Intel or AMD infotainment hardware.
  • Check the region: some features are country-specific; Emergency Drive Away is specifically listed for the US in the 2026.38.5.12 notes.
  • Check model year and trim: similar-looking cars may have different supported hardware.

How to check and install a Tesla software update

  1. Park in a safe place and connect the vehicle to reliable Wi-Fi when possible.
  2. Open Controls > Software to view the installed version and any available update.
  3. Read the release notes shown on screen. Check feature and model compatibility rather than relying only on a version number in a social post.
  4. Choose an installation time when the car will not be needed. Follow the prompts and do not interrupt the update.
  5. After installation, review new safety instructions or tutorials before using a feature.

Update timing can be staggered. If an update is not offered yet, keep the vehicle connected and check again later. Do not use unofficial files or attempt to bypass the vehicle’s normal update process.

Frequently asked questions

What is the latest Tesla FSD version in November 2026?

Release notes reviewed for this guide list FSD 14.2.2.6 with software 2026.39.6 and FSD 14.3.13 in an early 2026.38.5.12 rollout. The version available to your car depends on its model, hardware, region and rollout status.

Can I drive away while my Tesla is plugged into a Supercharger?

Emergency Drive Away is documented for emergency use only. Driving with the cable attached can damage the car and charging equipment. Never use it for convenience or as a normal way to end a charging session.

Does FSD Supervised mean my Tesla drives without me?

No. It remains a supervised driver-assistance system. You must pay attention and remain responsible for the vehicle at all times.

Will my older Model 3 or Model Y receive every new feature?

Not necessarily. Support varies by model year, hardware, trim, software build and region. Check the release notes displayed by your own vehicle.

Why has my Tesla not received the update yet?

Updates are rolled out in stages and can depend on vehicle configuration and region. A delay does not by itself indicate a fault. Check the Software screen and Tesla’s support information.

Bottom line for US Tesla owners

The November 2026 software releases show why owners should read the release notes for their own car. FSD 14.2.2.6 and 14.3.13 are distinct version references; Emergency Drive Away is an emergency-only function with potential hardware damage consequences; and FSD remains supervised driver assistance. Before using a new feature, read the release notes on your vehicle and follow Tesla’s instructions.
