federal EV tax credit status (Ended)

The federal New Clean Vehicle Credit of up to $7,500 and Previously-Owned Clean Vehicle Credit of up to $4,000 ended for vehicles acquired after September 30, 2025. An earlier qualifying acquisition may still be claimed subject to IRS acquisition and placed-in-service requirements and the applicable filing rules, including Form 8936. A new vehicle acquired after September 30, 2025 does not qualify. See the IRS Clean Vehicle Credits guidance and consult a tax professional about an earlier purchase.

Insurance, Maintenance and Depreciation

The Model S costs more to insure than a Model 3 or Model Y because of its price, performance and repair costs. Here is a realistic annual estimate for a US driver covering 12,000 miles per year.

  • Home charging: About 3,000 kWh per year at roughly $0.17/kWh, or about $510 annually.
  • Insurance: Roughly $3,000 to $4,500 per year depending on state, driver and coverage.
  • Maintenance: Tires, cabin filters, brake fluid checks and inspections, often $600 to $900 per year.
  • Registration: Higher luxury vehicle fees plus possible state EV fees.
  • Depreciation: Luxury EVs depreciate faster than mainstream models, so resale value matters over a long ownership period.

Charging at home is far cheaper than gasoline, but the savings are a smaller share of total ownership cost at this price point. Insurance and depreciation are the biggest ongoing expenses.

Is the Model S Worth It in 2026?

The Model S is worth it if you want flagship range, a quiet luxury cabin and performance that rivals far more expensive cars. If you mainly need practical family transport, the Model Y or the Model 3 deliver much of the Tesla experience for less money and may qualify for state or local incentives. No federal clean vehicle credit is available for a new 2026 purchase.

For buyers who want the sedan flagship and can absorb higher insurance, registration and depreciation, the Model S AWD is the better value, while the Plaid is a halo performance model.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a Tesla Model S cost in the USA in 2026?

The Model S starts around $79,990 for the AWD and about $94,990 for the Plaid before destination fees, taxes and options.

Is the federal EV tax credit status still available for new purchases in 2026?

No. The federal new and previously-owned clean vehicle credits ended for vehicles acquired after September 30, 2025. Earlier qualifying acquisitions may still be claimed subject to IRS requirements, including Form 8936. New purchases after that date do not qualify.

What is the out-the-door price of a Tesla Model S?

A base Model S AWD can land around $87,000 to $88,000 out the door in a state with about 7% sales tax and typical registration fees, while the Plaid can exceed $103,000.

What is the monthly payment on a Tesla Model S?

With about $10,000 down, a 60-month loan and a 6.5% APR, a Model S AWD can be roughly $1,520 per month and the Plaid around $1,840 per month.
