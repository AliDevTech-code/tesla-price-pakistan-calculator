Tesla Charging Cost in 2026: Home vs Supercharger Prices in the USA

A practical breakdown of home electricity, public fast charging, monthly budgets and the numbers behind a Tesla charging estimate.

Tesla charging cost in 2026 depends mainly on where you plug in, your local electricity price, how far you drive and how efficiently your model uses energy. For many US owners, charging at home is the lower-cost option for everyday driving. Superchargers offer speed and convenience on road trips, but their prices vary by location and sometimes by time of day.

This guide uses the latest EIA monthly residential electricity data available for August 2026 and explains how to calculate your own cost instead of relying on one nationwide “full charge” price. The image above is an illustrative comparison, not a guaranteed quote for every Tesla or charging station.

Quick answer: what does it cost to charge a Tesla?

The US average residential electricity price was 18.16 cents per kWh in August 2026, according to the U.S. Energy Information Administration. At that rate, an illustrative 75 kWh usable battery charged from empty with 10% extra electricity to account for home charging losses would use about 82.5 kWh from the meter and cost approximately $14.98.

That is a planning example, not a fixed Tesla price. Some states and utility plans are much cheaper or more expensive. Supercharger pricing is displayed for each location in the Tesla app or vehicle and can change by site, demand and time.

Simple formula:
Home charging cost = battery energy needed × (1 + charging-loss allowance) × your electricity rate.
For driving costs, use: kWh per 100 miles × electricity rate × 1.1.

Tesla charging cost per 100 miles

A useful comparison is cost per 100 miles, because it accounts for efficiency rather than battery size alone. Suppose a vehicle uses 30 kWh per 100 miles at the battery and home charging losses add 10%. At the August 2026 national residential average, the estimate is:

30 × 1.10 × $0.1816 = about $6.00 per 100 miles.

If a larger or less efficient vehicle uses 33 kWh per 100 miles, the same calculation is about $6.59. Cold weather, high highway speeds, large wheels, towing and aggressive acceleration can increase consumption. Gentle driving and moderate temperatures can reduce it.

Home charging vs Tesla Supercharger

Home charging is usually convenient for daily use because the car can charge while parked and the price is based on your electricity plan. Superchargers are designed for quick top-ups during travel. There is no universal Supercharger price: Tesla says each site’s rate appears on the vehicle touchscreen or in the Tesla app. Some locations use time-of-use pricing, and live-pricing sites can adjust rates based on occupancy.

As a market snapshot, reporting on Q3 2026 fast-charging prices cited an average Tesla Supercharger rate of about 56.9 cents per kWh for non-members and 39.6 cents for members. Those are reported averages, not a promise of the rate at a particular station. At 56.9 cents, 30 kWh for 100 miles would cost about $17.07 before any vehicle-specific adjustment—substantially more than the home example.

Why Supercharger prices change

Tesla explains that Supercharger prices differ by station. Certain sites offer peak and off-peak prices; some locations use live pricing based on occupancy. Your touchscreen or app shows the price before you start. The rate is set when the session begins, and congestion fees may apply if a vehicle remains connected after charging or above a site’s stated threshold.

  • Check the price on the station pin before navigating.
  • Look for off-peak windows where offered.
  • Move the car promptly after charging to avoid possible congestion fees.
  • Review the final session cost in the Tesla app’s Charging History.

How to lower your Tesla charging bill

  • Charge at home when practical. Use public fast charging mainly when you need the speed or are travelling.
  • Compare utility plans. An EV-specific or time-of-use tariff may offer lower overnight electricity rates, but check all fees and peak periods.
  • Schedule charging thoughtfully. Set a departure schedule or charging window that matches your utility’s lower-rate period.
  • Watch efficiency. Tire pressure, speed, temperature, roof racks and cargo all affect energy use.
  • Use the car’s energy screen. Compare actual Wh/mi and charging history rather than assuming every trip matches the EPA range.
  • Check station prices before a trip. A nearby Supercharger may cost more than another site a short distance away.

Does charging a Tesla cost less than gasoline?

It can, especially when most charging happens at home, but the answer depends on local electricity prices, gasoline prices and the fuel economy of the vehicle being compared. For a fair comparison, calculate the cost to drive 100 miles in both vehicles: Tesla kWh per 100 miles multiplied by your electricity price, versus 100 divided by the gas car’s MPG multiplied by the local price per gallon. Include public fast charging if you rely on it often.

Do not compare a cheap home-charging estimate with a gas vehicle’s worst-case fuel economy. Use your own utility bill, local gas price and realistic driving mix.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to charge a Tesla at home in 2026?

At the EIA August 2026 residential average of 18.16 cents per kWh, an illustrative 75 kWh usable battery with 10% charging losses costs about $15 from empty. Your local rate and battery size will change the figure.

Is a Tesla Supercharger more expensive than home charging?

Often it is, but rates vary. Check the exact station price in your Tesla app or vehicle before charging.

How much does it cost to charge a Tesla Model Y per month?

It depends on monthly mileage and efficiency. At 1,000 miles, 33 kWh per 100 miles, 10% charging losses and 18.16 cents per kWh, the estimate is about $66 per month.

Can I charge my Tesla using a regular wall outlet?

Yes, Level 1 charging from a suitable household outlet is possible, but it is slow. Follow Tesla’s instructions and use an appropriately rated, safely installed outlet. A dedicated Level 2 setup is faster for many drivers.

Where can I see the exact Supercharger price?

Select a Supercharger location on your Tesla touchscreen or in the Tesla app. Tesla displays site-specific pricing before the session begins.
