Tesla Battery Replacement Cost in USA (2026): Model 3 & Model Y

Published November 4, 2026 • Tesla Ownership Guide • Research-based estimates, not a service quote

For many electric-car shoppers, the biggest long-term question is not how often a Tesla needs an oil change—it is what happens if its high-voltage battery ever needs major work. Searching for Tesla battery replacement cost can produce wildly different numbers, especially for Model 3 and Model Y owners. Some pages quote a single price as if Tesla had a national menu. It does not.

Tesla does not publish one fixed US retail price for replacing every Model 3 or Model Y battery pack. The final estimate depends on the model year, battery chemistry and pack revision, whether the pack is new or remanufactured, the cause of failure, labor, parts availability and your location. This guide separates documented historical evidence from broad planning estimates, explains the warranty, and shows what to ask before approving an expensive repair.

Quick answer: For rough budgeting only, third-party 2026 estimates often place a complete out-of-warranty Model 3 or Model Y battery-pack replacement in a broad range around $12,000–$18,000 or more. This is not an official Tesla price or a guaranteed quote. Some repairs may cost less if a component-level repair is possible; unusual packs or extensive damage can cost more. Always request a written, VIN-specific estimate.

Tesla's public warranty documents explain coverage, but they do not provide a universal consumer price list for out-of-warranty battery replacement. Independent websites publish estimates, and owners sometimes share invoices, but those figures may describe different pack generations, repair scopes and dates.

What does a Tesla Model 3 battery replacement cost?

Model 3 battery packs have changed across model years and configurations. Standard Range and Rear-Wheel Drive cars may use different pack sizes and chemistry from Long Range or Performance versions. Even vehicles with the same badge can therefore have different parts and labor requirements.

One useful public reference is a reported 2020 Tesla Service invoice for a Model 3 AWD. The published breakdown listed a remanufactured 75 kWh battery pack at $13,500 and labor at $2,299.27, for a total of $15,799.27. That is a real historical example reported by Recurrent and discussed in reporting based on the original invoice. It is not a current 2026 price: the invoice is several years old, and parts, labor rates and pack availability can change.

When comparing a Model 3 quote, ask whether it includes the battery assembly, coolant and seals, labor, diagnostic time, taxes, shipping or core charges, and any required software configuration. Ask whether the replacement is new, remanufactured or otherwise refurbished, and what warranty applies to the replacement part and labor.

What does a Tesla Model Y battery replacement cost?

Model Y estimates also vary by year and battery configuration. Some Model Y versions share broad component families with Model 3, but that does not mean their packs, mounting systems, labor procedures or replacement costs are identical. Structural battery designs and different pack generations can affect how a repair is performed and which independent repair options are available.

There is no reliable public Tesla price list that guarantees a Model Y replacement amount. Treat online Model Y totals as estimates unless the publisher shows a recent, verifiable invoice for the same model year and pack. Ask the service provider to identify the exact pack part number and explain why the diagnosis calls for a full pack rather than a repair to a smaller component.

Tesla battery warranty: could replacement cost you nothing?

Before worrying about a five-figure bill, check the vehicle's original Battery and Drive Unit Limited Warranty. Tesla generally provides battery and drive-unit coverage for 8 years, with a mileage cap that varies by model and configuration. Many Model 3 and Model Y versions have a 100,000- or 120,000-mile limit; other Tesla models and configurations can have different limits. The time and mileage limits are whichever comes first, and the warranty booklet supplied for the specific vehicle is the controlling document.

Tesla warranty language for many configurations also includes a minimum 70% battery-capacity retention threshold during the applicable warranty period. This does not mean that every owner can request a free battery at 69.9% based on a phone estimate. Tesla assesses the claim using its warranty terms and measurement process. Range estimates are affected by temperature, speed, wheels, tires, driving style and other conditions, so a dashboard range number by itself is not a definitive battery-health test.

Warranty coverage is not the same as a promise to replace a pack for any reason. Physical damage, collision damage, misuse, unauthorized opening or servicing, and other exclusions may affect eligibility. Review the warranty for your vehicle identification number (VIN) and ask Tesla to explain the diagnosis in writing. Start with the official Tesla Vehicle Warranty information; then open the warranty document that matches your model year and configuration.

Does normal battery degradation mean you need a replacement?

No. Lithium-ion batteries gradually lose some capacity as they age and accumulate use. A small reduction in displayed range is not automatically a fault, and it does not automatically mean the pack is close to failing. Battery management software, temperature, recent driving and charging conditions can all influence the range estimate shown by the car.

Fleet research also suggests that full pack replacement is much less common than many first-time EV shoppers fear. Recurrent's research on EV battery replacements discusses how replacement frequency varies by vehicle age and model year. The important distinction is between gradual capacity loss and a diagnosed battery fault. Do not authorize a replacement solely because the car no longer displays its original EPA range.

Battery repair vs. full replacement: which is better?

A high-voltage battery is a complex assembly, not a simple consumer battery. Depending on the vehicle and failure, the issue may involve a sensor, contactor, wiring, cooling component, battery-management electronics, a module or the pack enclosure itself. A qualified technician needs to identify the actual fault before recommending a repair path.

  • Component-level repair: May be suitable when a specific serviceable component is responsible and the pack is otherwise safe and within repair specifications.
  • Module-level repair: May be possible for selected pack generations and faults, but not every pack is designed or approved for this approach.
  • Remanufactured replacement pack: Can be a lower-cost alternative to a new assembly when an appropriate unit is available. Confirm pack condition, compatibility and warranty.
  • New replacement pack: May be required when damage is extensive, the enclosure is compromised, or repair is not approved or safe.

Do not choose a shop on price alone. High-voltage battery work involves dangerous electrical energy and specialized lifting, isolation, diagnostic and handling procedures. Use Tesla Service or a qualified EV repair facility with appropriate training, equipment, insurance and a clear written warranty. Avoid anyone who proposes opening or modifying a pack without explaining the safety process and the effect on vehicle warranty coverage.

Signs your Tesla battery needs professional attention

There is no single dashboard message that proves a pack must be replaced. However, repeated high-voltage battery warnings, sudden and unexplained changes in usable range, charging problems, reduced power, coolant-related alerts or evidence of underbody impact deserve prompt attention. A warning can point to a system fault that is repairable without replacing the complete pack.

If the vehicle has been struck underneath, has visible battery-enclosure damage, or displays a serious high-voltage warning, follow the vehicle's alerts and contact Tesla Roadside Assistance or Service. Do not crawl under the car, open the battery enclosure, attempt a DIY high-voltage repair or continue driving if the vehicle warns you not to. Safety comes before getting another price estimate.

How to get an accurate Tesla battery replacement quote

  1. Confirm warranty status. Check the in-service date, odometer and exact battery warranty for the VIN.
  2. Request a diagnostic report. Ask what fault codes or test results support the recommendation.
  3. Ask whether a full pack is necessary. Request an explanation of any component or module repair that was considered and ruled out.
  4. Get an itemized estimate. Separate the pack, labor, coolant, seals, diagnostics, taxes, shipping and any core charge.
  5. Identify the replacement pack. Confirm new versus remanufactured, compatibility, part number and expected availability.
  6. Get warranty terms in writing. Ask how long the replacement part and labor are covered, and whether coverage transfers to a later owner.
  7. Compare a qualified second opinion. If you use an independent shop, verify its EV battery experience, safety procedures and insurance.

How long does a Tesla battery last?

There is no single expiry date for a Tesla battery. Its useful life depends on chemistry, temperature exposure, charging habits, mileage, time and the way the vehicle is used. Many owners will experience gradual range reduction rather than a sudden need for a complete replacement. The battery warranty period is a coverage limit, not a prediction that the pack will fail as soon as the warranty ends.

For everyday ownership, follow Tesla's charging recommendations for your specific battery and vehicle, keep software updated, and pay attention to unusual alerts. Avoid leaving a vehicle at an extreme state of charge for long periods when the owner's manual recommends otherwise. Do not obsess over every small range fluctuation; look for persistent changes and have concerns assessed professionally.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to replace a Tesla battery in 2026?

Tesla does not publish a universal retail price. Broad third-party planning estimates for a complete Model 3 or Model Y pack replacement often fall around $12,000–$18,000 or more, but the actual quote depends on the vehicle, pack, parts and labor. Get a VIN-specific written estimate.

Is a Tesla battery replacement covered by warranty?

A qualifying battery failure may be covered during the applicable Battery and Drive Unit Limited Warranty. Coverage duration and mileage caps depend on model and configuration. Normal degradation, physical damage and other exclusions are handled under the exact warranty terms.

Does Tesla replace the whole battery or just a module?

It depends on the fault, pack design and approved repair procedure. Some issues may be repaired at component level; others require a complete pack. Ask for the diagnostic basis of the recommendation.

Can an independent shop replace a Tesla battery?

Some specialist EV facilities perform battery diagnostics and selected repairs. Confirm that the shop is qualified for your exact pack, follows high-voltage safety procedures and provides written parts and labor coverage.

Will a Tesla battery fail after 8 years?

No. Eight years is a common warranty duration, not a battery expiration date. A battery can continue operating beyond the warranty, although capacity and repair risk vary with age, mileage and use.

Is a refurbished or remanufactured Tesla battery worth it?

It may reduce the upfront cost, but compare the pack's condition, compatibility, included components, installation price and warranty with a new option. A low price without a clear warranty can create more risk.

Final thoughts

The headline Tesla battery replacement cost can look intimidating, but it should not be confused with the normal cost every owner will eventually pay. Many battery concerns are not full-pack failures, and warranty coverage may apply when a qualifying defect occurs within the applicable period. The right decision starts with the vehicle's exact warranty, a professional diagnosis and an itemized quote—not a generic number copied from a forum.

If you own a Model 3 or Model Y, keep your warranty documents, respond to battery warnings and compare repair options only with qualified specialists. Use the estimates in this guide as a starting point for questions, then rely on a current written quote for your car.
